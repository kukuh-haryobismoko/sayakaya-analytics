    Who opens the buy form, and who pays

    People who opened the buy form in this period, grouped by what the main database knows about them, and how many paid a buy afterwards. Age and account age are as of their first open. Risk level is the result of the in-app risk questionnaire. Unknown: the profile field is empty.

    Where people open the buy form from

    The screen people were on right before the buy form opened, in the same app session. Ordered: created an order in that session. Paid: paid a buy within 3 days of opening the form from that screen. Going back to the form from checkout is not counted as a new open. Screens fewer than 3 people came from are hidden.

    Where people leave the buy flow

    App sessions that reached the buy form but created no order, by the furthest step reached. Next screen is where they went after their last buy-flow screen; "left the app" means nothing else happened in that session. Paid within 7 days: those people who came back and paid a buy within a week. Up to 12 exits per step.

    Orders by payment method

    Every buy order created in this period, by how the person chose to pay. Expired: the payment window closed unpaid. Waiting: not paid yet and not expired. Minutes to pay is the median from order to payment, for paid orders. Paid another order: people with an expired or cancelled order here who paid a different buy within 7 days.

    What people did before subscribing

    For each screen and action: of the logged-in people who did it in this period, the share who paid a buy, next to the share among people who did not do it. Subscribers only count what they did before their first paid buy. Lift 2.0 means twice the rate of those who did not. Active people do more of everything, so this shows what subscribers tend to do, not what makes them subscribe. Buy-flow steps are hidden by default because they are part of subscribing. Fewer than 20 people: hidden.

    Time to first subscription

    People who signed up in this period: days from sign-up to KYC verified and to their first paid buy. There is no end date, so recent sign-ups have had less time.

        Every completed sell in the period, app or not. Held: days since the person first bought (or switched into) that fund. Full: sold all units of the fund. Bought again: paid a new buy within 30 days, so recent sells have had less time. Holding nothing: no fund units today.

        What investors did before selling

        Investors who already had a paid buy before the period: for each screen and action, the share who sold, next to the share among investors who did not do it. Sellers only count what they did before their first sell. The way to the sell button and the PIN are hidden by default because almost every seller passes them. Fewer than 20 people: hidden.

        Switch flow, step by step

        People who opened the switch form in this period and how many reached each next step. Switched: a completed switch in the main database from their confirmation onward.

        Period

        Looks at what logged-in people use in the app and lines it up with what the main database says about them: holdings today, and buys and sells in the period. These comparisons describe who uses what. They do not show that a feature makes people invest.

        How the data connects

          Features and the people who use them

          Per feature: logged-in people who used it in the period, how many hold a portfolio today, their median portfolio value, and how many bought or sold in the period (at any point, not necessarily after using the feature). The first row is every logged-in app user, for comparison.

          Investors by portfolio size and app use

          Investors holding units they bought, today, by portfolio value and by how many days they opened the app in the period (logged in). Each cell: investors, and their portfolio value below. Institution accounts are left out.

          What people search for

          Whole search terms, lower-cased. Tapped: tapped a result in the same session within 30 minutes. Bought: paid any buy within 7 days of the search. Bought what they tapped: paid a buy of a fund they tapped. Terms fewer than 2 people typed are hidden.

          How people narrow down funds

          Sort choices on the fund list, the expert-picks theme opened, and the risk level people land on when the app asks for a risk profile before showing a fund. Paid: a paid buy within 7 days of first making that choice.

          Getting started

          This dashboard shows live data pulled directly from the database — there's nothing to configure, just pick a section from the menu on the left. A few things that apply everywhere:

          1. Date range — the "From / To" fields at the top of the page and the Apply button next to them control the time window for whichever dashboard you're looking at.
          2. Total row: breakdown tables (by fund, investment manager, income bracket, investor, or period) end with a Total row that stays visible while you scroll. Only columns that add up are totalled; averages, rates, and dates are left blank. In Largest funds by AUM, the total investor count comes from the database, so someone holding several funds is counted once.
          3. Menu (sidebar) — click the ☰ button top-left to collapse it to icons-only (desktop) or open/close it (on phone/tablet). Click a section title (like "Dashboards") to fold that group away if you don't use it often.
          4. Theme — top-right: Auto follows your device's light/dark setting automatically, or pick Light/Dark to fix it.
          5. Exporting — wherever you see CSV or Excel buttons, that downloads exactly what's on screen as a file.
          6. Your account — your name is in the box under the logo; Password lets you change it, Log out signs you out.
          7. Language — top-right: switch between English and Bahasa Indonesia at any time; your choice is remembered.

          Investor

          Portfolio (PWC)

          Look up one investor by their SID code, name, or email, then see exactly what funds they currently hold, how their money has grown over different periods, and their AUM history over time.

          Portfolio Explorer (GS)

          Same idea as Portfolio (PWC) — search for an investor and see their holdings — but from a second data source, organized by the investor's savings goals, and viewable as of any past date.

          Portfolio Explorer (Main)

          Same as Portfolio (PWC) — same as-of-date snapshot feature — but sourced from a corrected daily table (mi_fee_logs.portfolio_fix) whose average buy price is weighted by unit instead of averaged per lot, fixing a cost-basis bug in the original portfolio_with_code pipeline.

          Portfolio (TX)

          Investor lookup whose average buy price is computed directly from the transaction ledger, not from portfolios.initial_price — only completed buy/SWITCH_IN/reinvestment/transfer_in transactions affect the average; sells never change it. Supports an as-of date (reconstructed purely from transactions up to that date; bonus holdings only show live, since bonus_portfolios has no history). Lets you uncheck individual fund rows (e.g. dust balances) before exporting.

          Portfolio (SInvest)

          Same as Portfolio (TX), but every number is built from the KSEI/SInvest custodian feed (sinvest.trx_history) instead of the app's own transactions table — a second, independent source for cross-checking one investor's holdings and average buy price.

          HNWI

          "High-Net-Worth Individuals" — the list of investors holding within a chosen AUM range (you set the min/max thresholds) as of a chosen date, including each investor's risk profile (risk level, investment priorities, risk tolerance), with a total-across-everyone summary and an export. The per-fund breakdown below defaults to that same investor list; applying its own Min/Max fund AUM filter switches it to generate its own list by fund holding amount instead, independent of the filter above.

          Top investors

          Ranks investors by how much they put in (Subscribers), took out (Redeemers), or put in net of what they took out (Net deposit) over a day, a week, a month, or any custom date range. Pick how many to show (top 10 to 500). Each row shows the investor's SID, name, email, amounts, number of transactions, and their share of everyone's total for that period. Exports to CSV or Excel.

          User lifetime

          The same fee revenue as Revenue (PWC), but answered per investor instead of per fund: what each one earns the platform, next to how long they've been with us — when they registered, when they first bought, and how long they've stayed invested. Click any investor for their month-by-month, fund-by-fund breakdown.

          Dormant win-back

          Computed live from main.transactions, not a precomputed table. For every investor, each 14+ day gap between two completed buys is a "dormancy episode," grouped into 2 Weeks / 1 Month / 2 Month / 3 Month buckets; it "converts" if a later buy eventually closes the gap. Episodes past 180 days of silence are excluded, that's effectively churned rather than about to come back (the Forecast & churn tab's churn model covers that population instead). Shows conversion rate and revenue (median alongside average, since a handful of large transactions skew the average) per dormancy length, plus two exportable user-level lists: repeat buyers (lifetime buy activity of everyone who ever recovered) and time-to-convert (days between the last buy before each gap and the buy that closed it).

          Google Sheet export

          Every investor lookup above (Portfolio, Portfolio Explorer, Portfolio (TX)/(SInvest)) has a Google Sheet button next to its PDF buttons — one click adds a new tab, named with the investor and a timestamp, to one shared tracker spreadsheet, with the same investor info and holdings table as the "PDF (portfolio only)" export (no fund-performance breakdown), then opens straight to the new tab. Ask a superuser for the tracker sheet's link if you don't have it yet.

          Bulk export

          Every investor lookup above (Portfolio, Portfolio Explorer, Portfolio (TX)/(SInvest)) also has a "Bulk export" panel under its search box — add investors one at a time with the "+ Add" button next to a search result, or find everyone under one referrer_code/sales_code and add them all in one go. "Preview summary" shows the combined AUM, holdings totalled by fund, and each investor's own AUM, SID, name, email, phone, referrer_code, and sales_code for the current list, before you spend an export on it — that contact detail is shown here only, never written into the export files themselves. Once the list looks right, export it to CSV, Excel, or PDF: holdings only, same as Send e-statement & portfolio's portfolio send — no fund-performance pages, since this is a list of investors' holdings, not a full fund report. Combined mode makes one file (Excel puts one sheet per investor, PDF one page per investor); separate mode makes one file per investor and bundles them into a single .zip download. Capped at 50 investors per export.

          Operations & Transactions

          Users transactions

          Browse any investor's transactions by SID code, email, or name (partial match, required), with the buyer's contact info and fund name attached to every row: same join as Remisier transactions, but searched by investor identity instead of referrer/sales code. Refine further by transaction type, status, fund, or date range. Exports to CSV, Excel, or PDF.

          SInvest transactions

          Browse the raw KSEI/SInvest custodian feed (sinvest.trx_history) directly — the same source Reconciliation compares against, but row-by-row and filterable/exportable, for tracing a specific transaction or investor.

          Remisier transactions

          The underlying transaction-level detail behind the remisier sharing numbers, searchable and filterable.

          Reconciliation

          A daily check that the numbers line up across systems — use this to catch discrepancies before they become a real problem.

          Send e-statement & portfolio

          Search an investor, then email them their portfolio (holdings only, no fund performance breakdown) as of a chosen date, their monthly transaction e-statement for a chosen month, or both in one email. The compose step shows an editable subject and body (pre-filled with a default letter) before anything is sent. Each attached PDF is locked with the investor's date of birth on file (DDMMYYYY) as its open password, or left unlocked if we have no birthdate for them, and the default letter tells them the password format. Batch send sends the same documents to many investors at once: search by name (* works as a wildcard, e.g. budi*santoso), SID, email, or phone number and add investors one by one, and/or paste a list of emails or SIDs. Anything that doesn't match an investor is reported back, not silently skipped. "+ New schedule" turns this into a recurring send (daily, weekly, or monthly at a chosen WIB time) of the portfolio, the e-statement, or both. Creating a schedule needs a one-time code emailed to whoever sets it up. It runs until an optional end date. Resuming a paused schedule sends anything that came due while it was paused right away and refreshes its next run date. Every scheduled send is logged in the same history as manual sends.

          Send fund performance

          Emails the "Reksa Dana Update" PDF to a list of people. Choose which funds the report covers: all funds, one or more fund categories, or specific funds, plus the NAV date. Add recipients by searching name (* works as a wildcard), SID, email, or phone number and adding them one by one, and/or paste a list of emails. Each recipient gets their own email, so one bad address doesn't block the rest. Unlike Send e-statement & portfolio, this can go to any email, investor or not. It has its own send history, and "+ New schedule" sets up a recurring send with the same fund choice. Resuming a paused schedule sends anything that came due while it was paused right away and refreshes its next run date.

          Email recap

          Every email this dashboard sends, in one place: e-statements and portfolios, fund performance updates, scheduled sends, and account emails (invites, password resets, schedule confirmation codes, with the code itself never stored). Each email shows who it went to, its subject, category, where it was sent from (a manual send, a batch, a schedule), a plain description of what it carried, and who sent it. Amazon SES then reports back whether it was delivered, opened, or clicked, or bounced or got marked as spam; open and click rates are out of delivered emails. Filter by period, category, and where it was sent from; the summary by category and by subject works like a campaign report, and History on any row shows that one email's full record. Opens are counted when images load, so some mail apps over-count or under-count them. Emails sent before this tab existed are included from the send history, without delivery or open data.

          Revenue & Partners

          Revenue (PWC) / Revenue (GS)

          How much revenue/fees Sayakaya itself has earned, broken down by fund and investment manager, from each of the two data sources the platform tracks (PWC and GS).

          Campaign revenue

          What each promo campaign earned back. A buy settled with a promo code locks those units up, and this estimates the management fee they earn while they stay invested — until the investor redeems early, or sells them after the holding period ends. Shown next to the campaign's bonus payout so you can see which promos paid for themselves.

          Remisier sharing (GS) / (PWC)

          Calculates how much revenue-share is owed to remisiers (referral partners) based on the AUM they've brought in, from each data source.

          Marketing

          Marketing attribution

          From Adjust, the app's mobile attribution tool (own dataset, adjust_analytics.events). One row per acquisition channel: clicks, installs, and funnel milestones through to a completed payment (OTP verified, registration completed, KYC verified, order created, payment completed), plus the resulting revenue. Adjust fires threshold-tagged duplicate events for the same transaction (e.g. payment_completed_1M_plus alongside the plain payment_completed, same transaction ID) purely for ad-network bidding, so only the plain event names are counted, or payments would be counted 2 to 3 times over. Nearly all traffic in this data is "Organic"; most of the small named channels (social posts, blog links, search terms) show clicks with zero resulting installs.

          Referral program

          The Sep-Dec 2026 referral bonus program's eligibility report. A referral only counts if the invited friend's very first-ever transaction is a Sucor Asset Management fund purchase of at least Rp1,000,000 made with someone's referral code; the bonus (Rp25,000 to each side, in Sucorinvest Money Market Fund units) additionally requires that fund's units to stay unchanged for 30 days. Each row shows both people's contact details, the transaction, days held, and a status — Eligible, Pending (still within the 30-day hold), or Not eligible (with the specific reason, e.g. redeemed early or KYC not verified). An Inviter leaderboard sits above the row-level detail: one line per inviter with how many people they referred and how many transacted at all (any fund, any amount) — broader counts than the eligibility rule itself — next to how many of those referrals qualified, and of those, how many are Pending vs. Eligible. On this leaderboard, "Invited" only counts an invitee if they also registered during the period — an old referral relationship that never leads to a signup/purchase in this window doesn't clutter the list. Below the leaderboard, an Invited users table lists that same "Invited" population one row per person — name, registration date, SID, email, phone, their own referral code, the referrer code they signed up with, KYC status, and first-transaction status — separate from the row-level Referral detail table further down, which only covers invitees whose first-ever transaction met the campaign's own Sucor/≥Rp1jt rule.

          Referral program (KYC based)

          Same eligibility rule, same detail rows, and same permission-independent tab as Referral program above — the only difference is the Inviter leaderboard's "Invited" definition (and, correspondingly, which invitees show up in the Invited users table below it). Here, "Invited" is gated by the invitee's KYC verification date instead of their registration date (with the same 1-day grace window) — an invitee who was verified inside the period counts as invited even if they registered long before it, and one who's never been verified never counts, no matter how old the referral. Use this tab when you want "who's KYC-verified and in play for this campaign," rather than "who signed up because of this campaign."

          Referral activity

          Who referred whom across every user-to-user referral in the app, not only one program's ambassadors. Built from main.user_referrals (the immutable referral link, set once at referral time, unlike referrer_code which can be reassigned later) plus each referred investor's AUM as of a picked date, from mi_fee_logs.portfolio_with_code, same source and one-day correction as the HNWI tab. Referrers: one row per referrer, with how many people they referred, their first and most recent referral date, and the combined AUM of everyone they referred. Referrals: one row per referral, with the referral date and that investor's own AUM. No bonus amounts, tiers, or eligibility status, that program's exact rules aren't documented anywhere in this codebase, so this only reports referral activity and AUM, not what anyone is paid. Search by the referrer's SID, name, or email.

          Push delivery

          Firebase Cloud Messaging's own delivery log (own dataset, firebase_messaging.data): did FCM accept and hand off each push. This is send-pipeline health only, not an open or click rate. Firebase's standard export has no such engagement events for messaging, and the table carries only an FCM instance ID, not a Sayakaya user ID, so it can't be joined to revenue either. Shows send volume over a date range, a breakdown by platform (Android/iOS/unknown), and a breakdown by campaign (analytics_label), each with total sends, accepted, MISSING_REGISTRATIONS (a stale or invalid device token, the dominant failure reason), other errors, and the resulting delivery rate.

          Product

          App health

          Firebase Crashlytics + Performance Monitoring, Android and iOS combined (own datasets, one table per platform per product, unioned here since there's no combined table). Crashes: one row per distinct issue, platform, and fatal/non-fatal split, with event count, affected device count, and the latest app version seen. There's no "total active users" in Crashlytics' own export, so this can't compute a crash-free percentage, only raw counts. Performance: median duration (not average) per GraphQL query/mutation and custom screen-load trace, at least 20 samples. Screen-foreground dwell time and app-backgrounded time are excluded from this table entirely, Firebase logs those under the same "duration" field but they measure something unrelated to load latency, and backgrounded sessions especially can run for hours, which would otherwise wreck the average.

          Product funnel

          From Google Analytics (own dataset, analytics_266759216, one physical table per day, Feb 2026 onward). A cohort funnel, not a same-period count per step: it finds every device whose first Register click fell in the chosen date range, then checks, with no date limit on this half, whether that same device ever reached OTP submitted, KYC started, KYC verified, order created, and payment completed. Counting each milestone only within the chosen range independently would make the funnel look like it climbs partway through (otp_on_submit alone fires on every login, not just signup, so it would outnumber registrations), since a real person can register in one period and pay in a much later one. Split by platform (Android/iOS); each column only decreases moving right, within a platform. The milestone join scans GA4's full history regardless of the date range chosen, typically well under 1GB.

          User behavior

          Connects what people do in the app (Google Analytics events, which carry the investor's user ID after login) to the main database's transactions, holdings, and KYC status. For a chosen period it shows: logged-in app users per day and how many of them hold a portfolio or bought that day; app activity and buying by investor status (holding, redeemed, verified but never bought, not verified); which in-app actions come before a purchase within 7 days, compared with the average app user; each push campaign's opens and the buys that followed within 72 hours; fund detail pages viewed against buys of that same fund; and a follow-up list of people who started buying but never paid. Search any investor to see their app activity and transactions on one timeline, with their device, app version, and AUM; each lookup is recorded in the Activity log. Activity before login is not included, and it shows what buyers tend to do, not proof of what made them buy.

          Subscription analysis

          Follows logged-in people through the app's buy flow (Google Analytics) and checks each step against paid buys in the main database (completed, payment received, or verified; admin bonus buys excluded). For a chosen period it shows: a step funnel from opening the buy form to checkout, payment method, order created, and paid, split by platform, first or repeat buyer, or app version, with median minutes to order and to pay; which screen people open the buy form from (and the one before that) and how many of them pay within 3 days; where sessions leave the buy flow without an order and whether those people pay within a week; every buy order by payment method with expired, cancelled, and unpaid amounts; which screens and actions subscribers did before their first paid buy compared with people who did not (lift; buy-flow steps hidden unless ticked); days from sign-up to KYC and to the first paid buy for new sign-ups; the hours and weekdays when paid orders happen compared with app use; and the preset amount buttons on the buy form. Activity before login is not included, and the comparisons show what subscribers tend to do, not proof of what made them subscribe. A panel compares who opens the buy form and who pays by age, gender, occupation, investment purpose, risk level and account age from the main database. Each panel names the tables it joins and opens with a finding written from its own numbers, and a data map at the top shows how well the app's analytics match the main database for the chosen period.

          Onboarding analysis

          Starts from the main database: every account created in the chosen period (institution accounts left out). For each person it checks which KYC screens they reached in the app (KTP photo, selfie, personal data, occupation, address, bank account, signature, submitted), then the KYC review in the main database (verified first time, verified after a failed review, failed, waiting), the risk profile, and the first paid buy. It shows the biggest drop between steps, split by platform; minutes and app sessions to fill in KYC; hours to the review decision; which part of KYC was sent back; and how many verified people bought within 7 days. Each panel names the tables it joins and opens with a finding written from its own numbers.

          Redemption analysis

          Follows investors through the app's sell (redemption) and switch flows and checks each against completed sells and switches in the main database. It shows both flows step by step; every completed sell in the period by how long the fund was held, fund type, and full or partial, with how many sellers bought again within 30 days and how many hold nothing today; and which screens and actions investors used before selling, compared with investors who did not sell. Each panel names the tables it joins and opens with a finding written from its own numbers. The comparisons show patterns, not causes.

          Engagement analysis

          Lines up what logged-in people use in the app with what the main database says about them. It shows each feature group (search, sort and filter, watchlist, goal planning, calculators, notifications and more) with how many of its users hold a portfolio, their median portfolio value, and how many bought or sold in the period; investors by portfolio size and by how many days they opened the app, including how much money sits with investors who did not open it; the search terms people type, whether they tapped a result and bought that fund; and the sort choices, expert-picks themes and risk levels people pick. Each panel names the tables it joins and opens with a finding written from its own numbers.

          Event code tracking

          A generic, ready-to-use tab for tracking an event's own referral/sales code, built ahead of any actual event — no code or campaign has been set up yet, so pick whichever of users.sales_code or users.referrer_code the event ends up using, then type in its code(s). Shows a funnel (tagged with the code → KYC verified → transacted → transacted again), the list of tagged users, their transaction detail (own date range, exportable), and a cohort retention table with the grain — Day/Week/Month — picked at run time. Update this write-up once the real event's rules replace the generic funnel/cohort steps.
