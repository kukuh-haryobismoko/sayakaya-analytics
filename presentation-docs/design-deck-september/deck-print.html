03 — Acquisition, continued
Before they uninstalled: how far this cohort got
Distinct users who ever fired each event · Google Analytics, Aug 2026 uninstall cohort
Push didn't hold them either
Of the 138 people who received a notification, 78% dismissed at least one and 20% ever clicked one. In-app messages barely reached this cohort at all — only 7 people ever saw one, too small a sample to read into.
A promo didn't save the sale
Up to 12 people clicked into a promo screen at some point and still ended up in this cohort — same finding as July, a promo click alone isn't a retention signal.
Some left on purpose
12 people logged out manually and 1 cleared the app's local data before disappearing — a deliberate exit for at least this small group, same pattern as July.
Cohort is the August uninstall population traced in GA. This month's export is marginal per-event distinct-user totals, not a per-user ordered trace like July's — so the step rates above read as "at least X people reached Y," not a strict sequential funnel (a person could finish KYC without a recorded register_success, for example). GA still doesn't join to registration/KYC/buy in the warehouse, so these remain correlated events, not a proven causal order.

Still two things wrong at once
Cohorts are still shrinking: 89 new buyers in January became 18 in August. M+1 retention is sliding too, from a peak of 47% for May's cohort down every month since: 33% in June, 31% in July, 28% in August.
Dormancy got worse, not better
74.9% dormant over a year in July, 81.5% now. August's positive net flow ran on new and existing active money, it didn't reactivate anyone.
Why? A hypothesis, not confirmed
Recent cohorts are small (16-24 people), so a couple of accounts move the percentage several points. Two partial, testable factors: the slide overlaps the platform's own 8-month AUM decline (joining mid-slump may discourage adding more), and promo-code first-buyers repeat-buy less all year (29% vs 36% organic) while their share of new cohorts rose from 2% in March to 32-38% in May-June. Neither fully explains it alone.

Buying again ≠ staying invested
February's cohort re-bought only 41% of the time by M+1 on the previous slide, but 97% are still cumulatively net-positive here. Most cohorts hold 70-97% net-positive well past M+1.
January's Rupiah swings are 6 accounts, not 89
+Rp 204.7 B in January's own first month, then net-redeemed every stretch since (−Rp 233.2 B combined). 6 of 89 accounts drive nearly all of it, one alone moved +Rp 50 B. Read the percentages, not the Rupiah swings, as representative.
Why January's % is lower: fund choice, not just whales
51 of January's 89 (57%) chose a Sucor Asset Management fund first, retained at 61% by M+6 vs. 82% for everyone else in the same cohort, a real person-level gap. Sucor promos ran that month, but none show up on their actual transactions, likely the same promo-tracking gap flagged elsewhere in this deck.
